In 1945 a farmer in Upper Egypt broke open a clay jar and found thirteen leather-bound books. One of them was the Gospel of Thomas: 114 sayings of Jesus, with no miracles and no story around them.

But scholars had already been reading pieces of it for half a century. We follow the Gospel of Thomas from the rubbish heaps of Oxyrhynchus, where Grenfell and Hunt found Greek "Sayings of Jesus" in 1897 and 1903, to the jar at the foot of the Jabal al-Tarif near Nag Hammadi, and into the debate over how early it was written.
