# Sources — 003 The Gospel of Thomas

## Texts quoted on screen (public domain)
- B. P. Grenfell & A. S. Hunt, Logia Iesou: Sayings of Our Lord (Egypt Exploration Fund, 1897) — archive.org/details/logiaiesousaying00gren. Quotes: Logion 7 ("A city built upon the top of a high hill..."), Logion 5 ("Raise the stone..."); VO: Logion 6 (prophet / physician). Introduction: Behnesa 120 miles south of Cairo, rubbish heaps, leaf found at the very beginning of work on the town, 5¾ × 3¾ in., "not much later than the year 200".
- B. P. Grenfell & A. S. Hunt, New Sayings of Jesus and Fragment of a Lost Gospel (1904) — archive.org/details/newsayingsofjesu00unknuoft (text from newsayingsjesus00grengoog). Quotes: prologue ("These are the... words which Jesus the living (Lord) spake to... and Thomas"), Saying 2 ("Let not him who seeks..."); VO: Saying 1 ("shall never taste of death"), lilies fragment (P.Oxy 655). Return in February 1903, "curious stroke of good fortune", written on the back of a survey-list of land; Hippolytus quotation (Refut. v. 7); their conclusion that the Gospel of Thomas was not a suitable source.
- KJV, John 11:16 ("Thomas, which is called Didymus"), John 20:25 (paraphrased).

## Facts checked against
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Gospel of Thomas" (britannica.com/print/article/592815) — 114 sayings, Nag Hammadi 1945, mid-2nd century, parallels with the Gospels.
- Elaine Pagels, The Gnostic Gospels (1979), intro on PBS Frontline — December 1945, Muhammad Ali al-Samman, Jabal al-Tarif (150+ caves), sabakh, red jar almost a meter high, jinn/gold, mother burned papyrus, blood feud and fear of police, priest, Cairo dealers, Coptic Museum, Jung codex.
- Smithsonian NMAA essay on P.Oxy 1 — excavated 1896–97, Bodleian, published 1897, identified as Greek Thomas after Nag Hammadi.
- Wikipedia: P.Oxy 654 (sayings 1–7, British Library), P.Oxy 1, Gospel of Thomas (Codex II c. 340; photographic edition 1956; first English translation 1959; Hippolytus Ref. 5.7.20; Origen Hom. in Luc. 1; Cyril; almost two-thirds parallels; early vs late camps; Jesus Seminar; Greek logion 30 end = Coptic 77), Nag Hammadi library (12 codices + tractate of a 13th, 52 treatises, Coptic Museum, doubts about the discovery story, Pachomian and private-collector hypotheses).
- HeritageDaily (June 2024) — Infancy Gospel of Thomas fragment in Hamburg, clay sparrows, Lajos Berkes.
- Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 3.25.6 (Gospel of Thomas among rejected books).
