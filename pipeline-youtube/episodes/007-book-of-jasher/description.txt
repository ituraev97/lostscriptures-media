Twice, the Bible points its readers to a book that no longer exists: the Book of Jasher. It is cited for the day the sun stood still over Gibeon (Joshua 10:13) and for David's lament over Saul and Jonathan (2 Samuel 1:18).

What was the "Book of the Upright"? We look at the two poems it preserved, a possible third citation in the Septuagint, the rabbis' answers in the Talmud and Josephus's note on the long day. Then the books that later carried its name: the Hebrew Sefer ha-Yashar printed in Venice in 1625 and translated into English in New York in 1840, and the 1751 English hoax signed "Alcuin of Britain".
