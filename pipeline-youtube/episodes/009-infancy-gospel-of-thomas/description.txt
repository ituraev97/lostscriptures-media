A five-year-old Jesus shapes twelve sparrows from clay on the Sabbath, claps his hands, and they fly away. A boy who bumps into him falls dead. Three teachers try to teach him the alphabet. None of these stories is in the Bible. They come from the Infancy Gospel of Thomas, a book Christians copied and retold from Ireland to Ethiopia for more than a thousand years.

This is the story of what the book actually says, how the second-century bishop Irenaeus already knew its alphabet scene, why John Chrysostom called the childhood miracles inventions, how it differs from the Nag Hammadi Gospel of Thomas, and how its oldest known copy turned up in 2024 on a scrap of papyrus in Hamburg.
