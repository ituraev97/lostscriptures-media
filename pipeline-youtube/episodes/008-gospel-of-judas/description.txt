Around AD 180, Bishop Irenaeus of Lyon warned about a book called the Gospel of Judas. For eighteen centuries no copy was known. Then a leather-bound Coptic codex came out of a cave in Middle Egypt, spent sixteen years in a bank vault on Long Island, and fell into nearly a thousand pieces before scholars could read it.

This is the story of the Codex Tchacos, what the Gospel of Judas actually says, and why scholars still disagree about whether its Judas is a hero or a demon.
