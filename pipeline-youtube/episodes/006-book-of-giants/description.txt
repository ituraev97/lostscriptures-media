Among the Dead Sea Scrolls is a book that is not in any Bible: the Book of Giants. It tells the story of the Nephilim of Genesis 6, the sons of the Watchers, from their own side — their dreams of the coming Flood, their messenger to Enoch, and a giant named Gilgamesh.

We follow the book's strange double discovery: a single line in the Gelasian Decree about "the giant named Ogias" who fought a dragon; M. R. James's 1920 guess that it was a Manichaean book; the fragments from the ruins of Kocho on the Silk Road, published by W. B. Henning in 1943; and the Aramaic copies from Qumran identified by J. T. Milik. Then the story itself: Ohyah and Hahyah's dreams, the flight of Mahaway, Enoch's letter, Gilgamesh and Hobabish, the throne vision, and the end of the giants.
