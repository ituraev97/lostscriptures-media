# 004 The Apocalypse of Abraham — sources

## Text quoted on screen (public domain)
- G. H. Box with J. I. Landsman, *The Apocalypse of Abraham*, translated from the Slavonic (SPCK, London, 1918; 2nd impression 1919). archive.org `apocalypseofabra00boxg` (djvu text used for every quote: ch. 5 Barisat, ch. 8 voice, ch. 11 Yahoel, ch. 23 serpent).

## Facts and dating
- Box 1918, Introduction: Codex Sylvester (first half of 14th c., parchment, 216 leaves, Apocalypse on leaves 164–182); Palaea recensions turn the first person into the third; Greek intermediate; original Hebrew or Aramaic; date end of 1st / beginning of 2nd c.; ch. 29 "can only be regarded as a Christian interpolation"; note on "Ur" = fire.
- Wikipedia, Apocalypse of Abraham: 70–150 AD (after the Temple's destruction, ch. 27); six Slavonic manuscripts; Tikhonravov 1863; first English translation 1898 in the Improvement Era (from German); Bogomil interpolations suggested by Rubinkiewicz (disputed by Sacchi).
- Jewish Encyclopedia (Ginzberg), "Abraham, Apocalypse of": last decades of the 1st century; Hebrew or Aramaic; Christian interpolations; Bonwetsch 1897.
- Orlov, Marquette University: introduction, manuscripts, bibliography.
- KJV: Genesis 15 (sacrifice, birds of prey), Leviticus 16 (scapegoat / Azazel), 1 Kings 19 (Elijah, forty days to Horeb, still small voice).
