Genesis says almost nothing about Abraham before God called him. The Apocalypse of Abraham, a Jewish book from about AD 70–150, tells that story: the son of an idol-maker, a wooden god burning in the kitchen fire, and a journey with the angel Yahoel to the throne of God.

The book was probably written in Hebrew, but no Hebrew copy is known. It survived only in Old Church Slavonic, in medieval Russian manuscripts, the oldest from the 14th-century Codex Sylvester. We follow the text from Terah's idol shop to Mount Horeb, the bird called Azazel, the fiery throne and the vision of the Temple in flames, and then the road the book took to Russia.
